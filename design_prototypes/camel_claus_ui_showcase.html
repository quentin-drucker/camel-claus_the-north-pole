What we took from Megabonk (and other roguelites)

Observed in Megabonk

  • Riveted plates. Every panel is a dark slate-green plate: a lighter header band with the title, a double hairline border and a rivet in each corner. One panel recipe for menus, offers, quests, stats and results.
  • Slab buttons sized to their text. The menu buttons aren't one fixed width; they're staggered slabs with a light top bevel and a dark bottom lip, so the column looks hand-stacked.
  • Corner brackets for focus. Four L-shaped brackets jump to whatever is selected (buttons, panels, upgrade rows). This works the same with mouse and gamepad.
  • Upgrade offers are rows, not cards. Each row is filled with its rarity color, with the rarity name breaking the top border, a rarity-framed icon, the name, a gold level and one line in the form Stat: 2.4 → 3.4 with the new value in green. Banish, Skip and Refresh sit underneath as colored slabs, each with its count above.
  • The HUD has no panels. Outlined numbers sit straight on the world: a hairline XP bar across the top edge, time, currency and kills top-left, a flat red HP bar with centered numbers, weapon icons with "LVL n", a checkbox objective, a big timer top-center, "LVL 46" top-right, and an item strip along the bottom with ×n stacks.
  • Display type is a pixel blackletter with a dark outline. It reads as handmade and a little goofy, and it's used for everything from titles to stat lists. The logo is a separate fat white sans with a heavy outline.
  • Quests and stats are dense lists: per-category tabs with their own progress bars, completed rows turn blue with a check, and the reward shows in the last column.
  • The world is the backdrop. Menus float over a live, softly blurred 3D diorama, and the level-up screen adds light rays and a gem shower behind the panel.

Carried into Camel Claus

  • Every concept keeps Megabonk's information design: rarity-framed offers with before → after numbers, counted action buttons, the HUD layout, quest rows and the two-column stats list. Those are what make it feel responsive and legible.
  • What changes per concept is the material (tin plate, knit, ice, paper, pixels), the type, the motion and the composition, so A–E are real alternatives rather than recolors.
  • Rarity follows our art bible, not Megabonk's: rare = ice, epic = purple, evolution = gold. Purple stays reserved for danger and epic.
  • From other roguelites: Vampire Survivors' bottom item strip with stacks; Brotato's single-screen pause with build and stats side by side; Hades' slanted, tilted panels with big type (concept D); Balatro's tactile lift-and-tilt on hover (all concepts, on cards).
